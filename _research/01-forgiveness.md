---
title: "Forgiveness, memory, and emotions"
collection: research
permalink: /research/forgiveness
---

I aim to understand the relationship between memory, emotion, and forgiveness. 
Broadly speaking, forgiveness involves an emotional change from negative emotions 
toward less negative or even positive emotions, as well as a motivational change 
in responses toward the wrongdoer. Some authors suggest that these changes 
are tied to forgetting the offense. However, evidence from my own work indicates 
that people can overcome feelings such as anger and resentment, despite having 
vivid memories of what was done to them. Forgiving is definitely not forgetting.

My research adopts a cross-cultural approach, examining both a sample of victims of war 
in Montes de María, Colombia who have experienced severe wrongdoings (e.g., murder, 
kidnapping, torture, and massacres) and community samples in the United States. 
It is also methodologically pluralistic: I use a combination of in-depth interviews, 
physiological and behavioral assessments, and ethnographic methods to gather 
convergent evidence on how the emotional responses associated with memories of 
wrongdoing change over time.
