---
title: "Forgiveness, memory, and emotions"
collection: research
permalink: /research/forgiveness
---

Through my research, I aim to understand the relationship between memory, emotion, and forgiveness. 
Broadly speaking, forgiveness involves an emotional change from negative emotions toward less negative 
or even positive emotions, as well as a motivational change in responses toward the wrongdoer. 
Some authors suggest that these changes are tied to forgetting the offense. However, numerous examples 
challenge this notion, showing that people can remember wrongdoings while still releasing negative feelings 
such as anger and resentment. This is particularly evident in contexts of war, where severe wrongs 
have been committed: although victims remember these experiences, many are able to forgive those responsible.

My research adopts a cross-cultural approach, examining both a sample of victims of war in Montes de María, 
Colombia who have experienced severe wrongdoings (e.g., murder, kidnapping, torture, and massacres) 
and a community sample in the United States. I use a combination of in-depth interviews, 
physiological and behavioral assessments, and ethnographic methods to gather convergent evidence 
on how the emotional responses associated with memories of wrongdoing change over time.
